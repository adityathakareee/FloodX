# FLOOD-X — Data Quality & Integrity Validation Rules
**Phase 1, Stage 2 Specification & Operational Directives**
**Geographic Domain**: Pune / Mula-Mutha River Basin, Maharashtra, India

---

## 1. Quality Control Philosophy

In disaster decision-support systems, **transparent data provenance** and **semantic accuracy** are critical to life-safety. Bad telemetry or misinterpreted units will trigger false flood alarms or, worse, miss active inundation events.

FLOOD-X adheres to four fundamental quality invariants:
1. **Never fabricate artificial observations**: Missing timestamps must remain flagged as `MISSING` or `INCOMPLETE`. No synthetic data may masquerade as genuine sensor measurements.
2. **Never delete suspicious observations**: Dubious or anomalous measurements must be retained with the semantic flag `SUSPECT` or `INVALID` alongside audit notes explaining why.
3. **Explicitly derive transformed metrics**: When raw data is processed (such as computing increments or hourly sums from a cumulative bucket), the derived data must be explicitly labeled `DERIVED` and stored with references to the raw parent records.
4. **Geographic relevance must be verified and enforced**: Out-of-basin stations must never be silently assimilated into local flood models.

---

## 2. Standard Quality Flags

Every observation in `rainfall_observations`, `weather_observations`, and `river_level_observations` carries one of the following authoritative flags:

| Quality Flag | Semantics | Permitted Downstream Use |
|---|---|---|
| `VALID` | Passes all domain range checks, physical bounds, and timestamp sanity tests. | Direct ML feature engineering, hydrological modeling, alerting. |
| `DERIVED` | Calculated mathematically from valid raw measurements (e.g. 5-min increments, hourly accumulations). | ML modeling, feature generation, dashboard visualization. |
| `SUSPECT` | Exceeds typical physical thresholds, exhibits rapid anomalies, or lacks secondary sensor cross-validation. | Warning visualization; excluded from primary automated alert triggers without human confirmation. |
| `INVALID` | Physically impossible (e.g., negative rainfall, water level below ground zero, relative humidity > 100%). | Stored for sensor diagnostic logging; excluded from all ML features. |
| `MISSING` | Gap in expected temporal sampling interval. | Used for data completeness auditing. |
| `INCOMPLETE` | Partial sampling during an aggregation window (e.g., only 3 out of 12 five-minute intervals present for an hourly sum). | Flagged in metadata; normalized or discarded based on threshold. |
| `REGIONAL_REFERENCE_ONLY` | Geographically valid telemetry, but physically outside the local study catchment area. | Regional hydrological context only; strictly forbidden as a local Mula-Mutha stage trigger. |

## 3. Specific Domain Invariants & Audit Register

### 3.1 CWPRS Khadakwasla: 08:30 IST Bucket Resets vs Hourly Intensity

- **Source File**: `data/raw/rainfall/rainfall_tel_hr_cwprs_mh_sample.csv`
- **Issue**: `MISLEADING_HEADER_NAME` & `CUMULATIVE_BUCKET_RESET`
- **Severity**: `MEDIUM` & `INFO`
- **Problem**: The raw WRIS column header states `Telemetry Hourly Rainfall (mm)`. However, empirical inspection of 24,211 rows reveals:
  1. The sampling interval is **5 minutes** ($98.6\%$ of records), not 1 hour.
  2. The values are **monotonically increasing cumulative daily rainfall**, not discrete hourly rates.
  3. Every morning at approximately **08:30 AM IST (03:00 UTC)**, the gauge automatically resets to zero (or low baseline).
- **Peak Cloudburst Analysis (July 25, 2024)**:
  - 08:15 IST: 144.2 mm
  - 08:20 IST: 144.8 mm
  - 08:25 IST: **145.6 mm** (Peak 24-hr cumulative bucket)
  - 08:35 IST: **0.6 mm** (New daily bucket commenced after reset)
- **Operational Directive**:
  - The value of 145.6 mm must **never** be interpreted as an hourly intensity of 145.6 mm/hr (which would be an unprecedented catastrophic world-record cloudburst rate). It is the 24-hour storm total.
  - FLOOD-X computes derived increments: $\Delta R_t = R_t - R_{t-1}$ (or $R_t$ upon reset detection).
  - Derived hourly accumulations are generated by summing increments over clock-hour intervals.

### 3.2 CWC Telemetry: Ankali Bridge Sangli Geographic Displacement

- **Source File**: `data/raw/river_levels/rwl_tel_hr_maharashtra_sw_007_sample.csv`
- **Issue**: `GEOGRAPHIC_DISPLACEMENT`
- **Severity**: `HIGH`
- **Station**: Ankali Bridge (Station Code: `MAHARASHTRA_SW_007`)
- **Coordinates**: Latitude 16.8028° N, Longitude 74.5664° E
- **Basin / River**: Mainstem Krishna River, Sangli District, Maharashtra
- **Distance to Pune**: **205.3 km South-Southwest**
- **Problem**: Although provided under Maharashtra surface water telemetry, Ankali Bridge is in a completely different river sub-basin over 200 km downstream/south of the Mula-Mutha confluence. Water levels at Ankali Bridge (~526–546 m MSL) have zero physical bearing on flash flooding along the Mutha River at Deccan Gymkhana or Sangam bridge in Pune.
- **Operational Directive**:
  - All 32,655 records are classified as `geographic_relevance = 'REGIONAL_REFERENCE_ONLY'`.
  - Under no circumstances will Ankali Bridge river levels be used as an input to Pune local inundation thresholds or evacuation triggers.
  - The frontend displays an alert badge highlighting this regional reference status.

### 3.3 Copernicus GLO-90: Digital Surface Model (DSM) Bias

- **Source File**: `data/processed/elevation/copernicus_glo90_pune_basin_dem.tif`
- **Issue**: `DIGITAL_SURFACE_MODEL_BIAS`
- **Severity**: `LOW`
- **Problem**: Copernicus GLO-90 is a Digital Surface Model (DSM), not a bare-earth Digital Terrain Model (DTM). The elevations reflect the top of tree canopies and tall urban structures in Pune. In high-density urban areas, DSM elevations may overestimate ground levels by 5–25 meters, potentially skewing surface runoff routing.
- **Operational Directive**:
  - Topographic slope layers are derived using moving window gradients to smooth localized building noise.
  - Phase 2 hydrological feature engineering must apply road network masking and hydro-flattening before computing flow accumulation networks.

### 3.4 OpenStreetMap: Evacuation Road Network & Shelter Certification

- **Source File**: `data/processed/gis/osm_features_pune.gpkg`
- **Issues**:
  1. `ARTERIAL_ROAD_NETWORK_ONLY` (`MEDIUM`): The evacuation network (906 segments) is filtered to arterial backbone highways (trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary). Local residential alleys and informal settlement lanes are omitted and must not be assumed navigable in micro-evacuation routing.
  2. `UNVERIFIED_SHELTER_CERTIFICATION` (`MEDIUM`): Civic and community facilities (7 points) represent unverified social halls and sabhagruhas from OSM. They have not been formally surveyed or certified by Pune Municipal Disaster Management authorities for flood-resistance, power backup, or sanitary capacity.

### 3.5 CHIRPS v3: Non-Continuous Monsoon Peak Temporal Sequence

- **Source File**: `data/processed/rainfall/chirps_v3_pune_daily_2020_2024_timeseries.csv`
- **Issue**: `TEMPORAL_RESOLUTION_LIMITATION`
- **Severity**: `LOW`
- **Problem**: The dataset contains 25 targeted daily observations representing severe monsoon peak epochs across 2020–2024 (e.g. July 2024 flood sequence), rather than a continuous 1,826-day time series.
- **Operational Directive**:
  - Daily time series models must treat this dataset as multi-epoch episodic training samples rather than a contiguous lag-autoregressive series.
